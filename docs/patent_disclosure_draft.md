### 1. ABSTRACT OF THE INVENTION
A system, method, and non-transitory computer-readable medium for supervisory safety filtering in robotic manipulation. A robotic end-effector equipped with an optical elastomeric tactile sensor interacts with an environment. A high-frequency (500 Hz to 2 kHz) safety supervisory filter intercepts unconstrained actuator commands generated by a primary policy (such as a neural network, diffusion model, or teleoperation feed) and solves a convex quadratic program subject to Control Barrier Functions (CBFs). The barrier functions are parameterized by physical contact invariants extracted from the continuous 3D force distribution field of the tactile sensor: the Traction Center of Pressure (CoP), the Contact Constraint Tensor ($\mathbf{C}$), and the Incipient Stick Ratio ($\rho_{\text{stick}}$). The system mathematically guarantees normal force upper bounds, prevents two-point wedging/jamming force couples during tight-clearance insertion, and enforces asymptotic Lyapunov passivity via an Energy Tank during non-smooth contact transitions without requiring retraining of the primary policy.

### 2. BACKGROUND & PRIOR ART SHORTCOMINGS
1. **Unconstrained Neural Manipulation Policies (e.g., Diffusion Policies, FARM, ACT):**
   Prior art models clone human teleoperation demonstrations. However, when deployed in contact-rich tasks under geometric misalignments, out-of-distribution (OOD) contact states cause violent actuator commands, force spikes exceeding material limits, part damage, or permanent wedging.
2. **Wrist-Mounted 6-Axis Force-Torque Sensors:**
   Conventional industrial systems monitor forces via load cells mounted between the robot wrist and tool. Due to contact ambiguity, wrist sensors cannot distinguish between single-point chamfer sliding and two-point jamming force couples, leading to false triggering and insertion failure.
3. **Passive Compliance (Remote Center of Compliance - RCC):**
   RCC mechanisms are passive spring structures calibrated for a single part geometry and length. They cannot adapt to varying workpiece shapes or dynamic slip conditions.

### 3. DETAILED TECHNICAL DESCRIPTION

#### 3.1 Tactile Contact Invariant Extraction Engine
The elastomeric sensor (GelSight Mini) measures a continuous 3D force distribution field over contact domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$:
$$\mathbf{f}(u, v) = [f_x(u, v), f_y(u, v), f_z(u, v)]^T \quad \text{for } (u, v) \in \Omega$$

The invention computes four mathematical invariants in real time:
1. **Total Contact Force & Contact Normal:**
   $$\mathbf{F}_c = \iint_\Omega \mathbf{f}(u, v) \, du \, dv, \quad \hat{\mathbf{n}}_c = \frac{\mathbf{F}_c}{\|\mathbf{F}_c\|_2}$$
2. **Traction Center of Pressure ($\mathbf{r}_c$):**
   $$\mathbf{r}_c = \frac{\iint_\Omega [u, v]^T f_z(u, v) \, du \, dv}{\iint_\Omega f_z(u, v) \, du \, dv}$$
3. **Contact Constraint Tensor ($\mathbf{C}$):**
   $$\mathbf{C} = \iint_\Omega \left( [u, v]^T - \mathbf{r}_c \right) \left( [u, v]^T - \mathbf{r}_c \right)^T f_z(u, v) \, du \, dv \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$$
   The eigenvectors of $\mathbf{C}$ define the principal directions of environmental geometric constraints (edges, grooves, chamfers).
4. **Incipient Slip Field & Global Stick Ratio ($\rho_{\text{stick}}$):**
   At each grid cell, local Coulomb margin is $\rho(u, v) = 1 - \frac{\sqrt{f_x^2 + f_y^2}}{\mu f_z + \epsilon}$. The aggregate stick ratio is:
   $$\rho_{\text{stick}} = \frac{\iint_\Omega \mathbb{I}(\rho(u, v) > \epsilon_{\text{stick}}) \, du \, dv}{\iint_\Omega \mathbb{I}(f_z(u, v) > 0) \, du \, dv} \in [0, 1]$$

#### 3.2 Control Barrier Function Formulation (CBF-QP)
The supervisory filter intercepts nominal policy torque $\mathbf{u}_{\pi}$ and solves:
$$\mathbf{u}^* = \arg\min_{\mathbf{u} \in \mathcal{U}} \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}_{\pi}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^2$$
subject to:
1. **Normal Force Protection Barrier:**
   $$h_{\text{force}}(\mathbf{x}) = F_{\max} - \hat{\mathbf{n}}_c^T \mathbf{F}_c \ge 0 \implies \dot{h}_{\text{force}} + \alpha_1(h_{\text{force}}) \ge 0$$
2. **Anti-Jamming Force-Couple Barrier:**
   $$h_{\text{jam}}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\|\mathbf{F}_{\text{insert}}\|_2}{\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{tilt}}\|_2 + \epsilon} - \gamma_{\text{jam}} \ge 0 \implies \dot{h}_{\text{jam}} + \alpha_2(h_{\text{jam}}) \ge 0$$
3. **Slip Prevention Barrier:**
   $$h_{\text{slip}}(\mathbf{x}) = \rho_{\text{stick}} - \rho_{\min} \ge 0 \implies \dot{h}_{\text{slip}} + \alpha_3(h_{\text{slip}}) \ge 0$$
4. **Energy Tank Passivity Constraint:**
   $$\frac{1}{2} \dot{\mathbf{q}}^T \dot{\mathbf{K}} \dot{\mathbf{q}} \le P_{\text{tank, available}}$$

### 4. PATENT CLAIMS

#### CLAIM 1 (Independent Method Claim - Tactile Safety Filter)
A computer-implemented method for controlling a robotic manipulator during contact-rich tasks, comprising:
(a) obtaining, from an elastomeric tactile sensor mounted on an end-effector of the manipulator, a multi-dimensional force distribution field across a contact interface;  
(b) calculating, using one or more processors, a spatial contact constraint tensor and an incipient slip metric from said force distribution field;  
(c) defining at least one Control Barrier Function (CBF) constraint on actuator inputs of the manipulator, wherein said CBF constraint establishes a forward-invariant safe set bounded by said contact constraint tensor and said incipient slip metric;  
(d) receiving a nominal control command from an unconstrained policy module;  
(e) solving, at a frequency of at least 500 Hz, an optimization problem to generate a safe control command that minimizes a deviation from said nominal control command while satisfying said at least one CBF constraint; and  
(f) commanding actuators of the robotic manipulator in accordance with said safe control command.

#### CLAIM 2 (Independent System Claim - Anti-Jamming Mechanism)
A robotic insertion system configured to prevent mechanical jamming during assembly, comprising:
a robotic manipulator having one or more articulated joints;  
an end-effector comprising at least two opposing grasping members, at least one grasping member equipped with an optical elastomeric tactile sensor;  
a controller communicatively coupled to the manipulator and the sensor, the controller configured to:
  measure a distributed traction field generated on the tactile sensor by a grasped workpiece interacting with a receptacle;  
  invert the distributed traction field to detect the presence of a two-point contact force couple between the workpiece and the receptacle;  
  compute a real-time jamming index based on a ratio of insertion force to tilting torque; and  
  in response to the jamming index traversing a predetermined threshold, project joint actuator commands into a null-space of forward insertion progress to decouple the two-point contact force couple without terminating task execution.

#### CLAIM 3 (Independent Apparatus Claim - Energy-Tank Multi-Rate Stabilizer)
A multi-rate robotic control apparatus for stabilizing contact transitions, comprising:
a low-frequency tactile perception module configured to infer contact compliance parameters from optical tactile images at a first frequency between 10 Hz and 50 Hz;  
a high-frequency torque control module configured to generate joint actuator torques at a second frequency between 500 Hz and 2000 Hz;  
an energy tank observer configured to track virtual energy stored and dissipated in the robotic control apparatus; and  
a dynamic stiffness limiter configured to constrain a time-derivative of a Cartesian stiffness tensor such that virtual energy injection resulting from perceptual latency at the first frequency does not exceed a stored energy balance in the energy tank observer, thereby enforcing Lyapunov passivity across high-stiffness environmental contact boundaries.
